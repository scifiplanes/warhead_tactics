# Warhead tactics

Present tense. Deltas → `docs/changelog.md`.

## Product

Mobile web survivors run. The deck is the run's airdrop pool. The Lead steps off alone, and octacopter cargo drones bring the cards in. They can hold a path into another field, or reach the LZ, arm the flare, wait out the craft, and load one by one. An empty deck fields one Cell. The first Cell is the Lead. A section with no living unit loses. A hull or a sentry does not keep it. The player builds a counted deck (cap 12) and picks Light, Standard, or Heavy. The Cell is free. Every other researched card is paid in credits for that deployment. Taking the same card again, in one deck or on the next round, raises that price by its base for each step. A round that leaves the card out cools one step. Unknown cards stay hidden until a field find. A find fades in while it moves into place. Squad pickup auto-fits gear onto a living section body (nearest bare hand / empty attach / fit first). Field finds bank into the research list. A successful extract unlocks only what boarded the craft (fitted gear, boarded hulls/units, unspent toolbar) — each haul grants one free RECOVERED deploy copy. Seen or abandoned kit stays research-only until paid. A wipe only banks finds for research. Pending research / MORE copies sit behind a count on the deck: a sheet groups by kind, affordable first. Research and extra copies cost twenty times the deploy price (floored at a rifle's price) per step, up to the deck cap. Credits come from kills, the Purge and Haul bounties (a finish reads 8/8 or 3/3), and salvage, intel, and samples. Extract banks that haul, pays 40 more, and pays back the deployment cost of each unit, vehicle, and fitted gear that boarded. Section gear pays back when someone boards. A wipe banks none. Body gear is pulled out of the shuffle so the Lead wears it at step-off; units, hulls, and section tools airdrop in shuffled order, each a walk from the squad and from the last one, and the next waits. A clock, this-run CR, pause, a Lead kit strip, a roster with kit marks, a nine-slot toolbar, and an end tally sit on the field. One campaign save, which can be restarted. A system menu sets thud volume and mute. A debug menu sets the field dither: levels, spread, scale, size, pattern, strength, cut, ink, blend, and whether it sits on the mud or the whole field. Opening the deck rolls a seed. The main menu has DECK and WORLD tabs; WORLD reads the campaign field graph. Travel stays path/LZ on the field.

## World

Three Uluses already annihilated each other. Residue is rad, burn-chem, bio, and AI-oxide. The campaign holds a seeded hex field graph (`src/meta/worldmap.ts`, disk radius 7 → 169 cells): each hex keeps abstract feral/rust/bio/oxide/scar floats, up to two unpicked salvage/intel/sample marks, and quiet sleepers. The WORLD tab paints each cell with ground terrain art (`hexTerrain` in `src/view/sprites.ts`). Paths aim along hex neighbor bearings. Only the hot field instantiates bodies (hostile cap unchanged). Leaving a node folds living hostiles into floats and scars the mud; clearing matters — scar slows packs (never empties a node forever), oxide tips Rust. Cold nodes eco-tick between visits. Deck airdrops stay this run's paid queue. The section can field Cells, Aegis, Forge, Wisp, an engineer, a relay, a recondog, a copter, a hunter-killer, a biohawk, a middlestrike, a cyberassassin, a cyberoperator, a JTAC, medic, jeep, bike, buggy, hoverbike, MRAP, HEX scout, HEX line, HEX siege, APC, plow, tank, truck, technical, SUV, steed, cart, mule, MLRS, IFV, MBT, an unmanned UGV, an unmanned leashed gunboat, a 60mm, drill, pump, and cutter. A rifle, SMG, shotgun, railgun, MG, sniper, grenade, mortar, blade, bow, or handgun is gear with a primary or secondary hand. A soldier wears one of each. Combat picks by range and whether that weapon can fire now. Optic (dot, holo, scope, long, reflex, therm sight, laser sight), grip, mag, brace, and muzzle cards fit the primary; a new primary dumps them on the mud as themselves, a short walk out. Deck body gear outfits the Lead at step-off, then rides each later unit or hull airdrop already fitted; orphan gear and section tools still drop as crates with their gear mark on the box. Field finds and shed parts stay pickups. Plate and stim stay on the soldier. The Lead starts with a rifle. A drop with no gun is issued one: a rifle for a soldier, a light mount for a hull or a follower. Androids keep their own gun. The first Cell is the Lead. Ferals and Rust fight the section and each other: shambler, doghead, pighead, goathead, crowhead, mutant, drone, walker, scoot, tract, coil, flake, roller, kite, slab, reach, mast, dray, rust plow, post, bloom, cluster, fog, ball, amalgam. A goathead may bring shamblers; a crowhead may bring dogheads. Coil, bloom, fog, and amalgam keep their own stacks. A rust sleeper hull sits quiet until the squad digs it, then wakes and leaves salvage. Marsh bogs a hull. A hoverbike does not. A stash is a short hold that opens a find. A field find may be a holdout survivor. Until a run has offered one, a stash may turn up a named shell — KADE VOS, MOTHER STEN, or WREN TAVE first — then a random named soldier. Extract keeps that soldier. Death removes a veteran, and redeploying one costs 80. Art: dense hand-authored pixel illustration on every surface — `docs/design/art.md`. Field footprints match shipped sizes (infantry 16×13, hulls/props as in art.md); densify inside the grid only; zoom 4.5/6. Each deck hull has its own stamp (drill auger, plow blade, tube mortar, steed pads, …). Goathead and crowhead keep horned/beaked flesh mass; section air (copter, hawk, mid) and rust scoot/kite/flake read apart from the base drone. High-angle foreshortened mass; stand-C coats; rotating gun wedge on friendlies; ferals dirtier. Scarce gold/blood/oxide. Motion: `docs/design/anim.md`.

## Loop

One move stick. Press and hold the left of the field to move. Hold Q or Space to group and E or Shift to spread. The pause menu lists the keys. Keys 1-9 fire the toolbar slots. A cooling slot drains and shows a gold count. Units auto-aim. Group closes the file in a glide, then boards one body per tap or hold-tick, alternating vehicles. The Lead takes the fastest hull. Spread disembarks one body before the file opens in a glide. The camera stays on the commander and eases well out as the file opens. Button labels show the next step. Bracket labels mark fire, boarding, finds, and loot. Red is a squad KIA or a lost part. An enemy down-label is gray and reads DOWN. Steel EXIT chevrons (numbered when several) and a gold LZ pad mark travel intents; edge plates and hold rings show the countdown. A gold column is the flare, and the craft, a large brick-shaped cargo VTOL, descends onto it. An inbound drop descends on its flare under an octacopter and names the card on the flare. The Lead steps off wearing leading deck kit. A soldier or a hull hangs on the cables and is set down already wearing its deck kit. Section tools and orphan gear hang as a crate with the gear mark on the box, and a plate names the gear while it is in view. Shed gun parts sit as themselves. The empty drone climbs away. Salvage, intel, and samples sit on the ground with their own plates. A relic is a find, and it pays no credits. Pickup is a short reach: a gold flash, a named bracket, and a gold toast; new cards read FIND with the name. The HUD kit strip shows the Lead's fitted guns and body gear and pulses when that kit changes; the roster repeats kit marks per body. A body that has not joined holds at the drop and shoots. Edge marks also point at empty hulls, stragglers, and Tracker hostiles. A corpse is that figure laid flat under the living file. A part is that figure's head, limb, wheel, or turret on the mud. A plate names it while it is in view. A dim line drifts in the quiet. Each shot throws a short hard-pixel muzzle flash at the gun tip — shape marks the weapon, and the flash is a real night light (punches the dark; section flashes briefly light sight like a beam; early rust tasers are a short pale zig-zag; later rust spit is a smaller oxide dot; blades and bows stay silent). Section powder (hip and set) cracks a muffled brass pop; hits land as muffled distant thuds. Hits tear off a limb or a wheel and throw it. A turret stays on the hull until the hull is badly hurt. Dead enemies stay on the ground. Shots, blasts, and breaks leave splatters, burns, and craters. A joined unit stays on foot and boards after a short delay when everyone else is mounted and a seat is free. Driving off leaves bodies behind. A Cell sets a medium gun for clean fire; on the move that rifle or shotgun hip-fires with worse miss and a slower semi. Standing still still waits out the set. Powder guns run a magazine and auto-reload when empty (mag cards add rounds); blades and bows do not. Heavy guns still need a full deploy. While a mag seats, the body tips the gun and feeds a chip, steel brackets hold a RELOAD plate, a bar fills under the health pip, and the kit strip shows the round count or RELOAD. Aegis, the APC, and HEX siege shoot on the move. A fitted grenade or mortar lobs. Feral bites land on a hull's skin and scrape it. A hull at 0 hp is dead. A goathead shoots. A crowhead lobs gnats. Early Rust (drone, scoot, kite) close with short tasers that hitch-stun (brief, no refresh lock); later Rust guns throw a straight bolt that can miss. Flakes come in a pack. A coil bites. Crewed hulls steer, accelerate, brake, and reverse. A moving hull rams harder and shoves. A hull at rest takes no collision damage, and a bump does not stall it. A second crewed hull rides beside the lead, clear of its skin. A crewed hull shoves an empty one aside. The stick stays with the Lead. If the Lead is down, it passes to a soldier, or to a follower when no soldier is left. A body still outside after the craft is down is left behind. Vehicles take hull damage. The plow and the tank break props. A Forge and an engineer dig cover down. A medic cannot patch an android. A Wisp opens the section's sight. A living relay brings the craft sooner. A hoverbike ignores mud. A light hull takes one light scrape, then skirts the cover. A stutter of ruins does not stack those scrapes. Body kit auto-fits: plate, stim, plating, ram. Gun slots auto-fit optic, grip, mag, brace, muzzle. Section tools: frag, mines, sentry, kit, radio, nitro, CAS, gunship, rocket, bomber, laser, NVG, thermal, thermite, EMP, smoke, stun, FPV. An exo frame fits a soldier. A jetpack ignores mud. A filter ignores weather. A Tracker paints blank brackets on every living hostile while its wearer lives, through night and smoke. A litter lets the bearer patch. An axe chews cover. An AT blast punches armor. A living operator jams Rust. A living JTAC tightens CAS, radio, FPV, and the sortie airframes, and stretches their station time. A laser paints a crowd for the next of those strikes. FPV is an over-the-horizon inbound, not a squad launch. A recondog carries night sight. A copter and a hunter-killer follow the Lead and do not board. Gunship, rocket, and bomber are crate call-ins: pickup puts them on the toolbar, a press brings one bird in from off-screen for a short orbit, then it flies out (no wreck) while the slot cools; one bird of that kind at a time. Airframes stay aloft with a ground shadow and pull their circle toward a nearby fight. Rocket and bomber lob; the gunboat and gunship strafe. The gunboat stays a leashed unmanned hull. The UGV follows and shoots with nobody aboard. Thermite ignores armor. EMP bites only Rust. Smoke hides the squad from ranged hostiles. Each node fixes ground and bearing; pressure and sky follow residue and depth. Several path exits fan from hex bearings; the LZ sits in the clearest gap from them. Holding any exit travels into that neighbor; holding the LZ only extracts (never travels). Exit, LZ, and stash holds keep their progress while any living section body stays in the ring (Lead dying mid-hold does not reset). Weather snaps between that pair. It slows the file and cuts sight. Night fades in and cuts sight further. A flesh soldier on foot throws a beam along his shot, and that beam sees at day range. A section muzzle flash is a brief omnidirectional light with the same day-range sight; hostile flashes still punch the dark on screen but do not light combat sight. A hostile in the beam or section flash light shoots back at day range. Androids carry no light. NVG and thermal shut the beams, and daylight shuts them. A living android carries thermal. CAS is a gunship pass along a crowd.
